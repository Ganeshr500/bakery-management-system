PROBLEM STATEMENT
=================

The problem
-----------
A lot of small bakeries still keep track of stock and sales in a paper
register or a basic spreadsheet. It works until it doesn't: nobody is sure
how many croissants are really left, an order gets accepted for something
that already sold out, and simple questions like "what sells the most?" or
"what are we about to run out of?" take ages to answer.

BakeryHub is my attempt at a small, reliable, script-based tool that keeps
inventory and orders consistent and answers those questions on demand. It
uses only what we learned in the first semester of Python, plus sqlite3
from the standard library.


Scope
-----
BakeryHub is a single-shop, console-based app meant for one bakery's
owner or counter staff.

What it does:
  - keeps a saved catalogue of items (id, name, category, price, stock)
  - takes customer orders: checks stock, deducts it, works out the bill
    and records the sale
  - cancels an order and puts the stock back
  - shows low-stock alerts, revenue numbers and best sellers

What it does not do (on purpose):
  - multiple branches or shops
  - a website or a GUI
  - online payments
  - a separate database server (data lives in one local SQLite file,
    data/bakeryhub.db)


Who uses it
-----------
Main user : the bakery owner / counter staff who manage stock and enter
            orders.
Indirect  : customers, whose purchases get recorded and billed.


Main features
-------------
1. Inventory management - add, view, restock, change the price of, and
   delete items.
2. Order processing - multi-item orders with automatic stock checks and
   deduction. If any line fails, nothing is deducted. Cancelling an
   order restores the stock.
3. Reports - low-stock alert, revenue summary (total and average order
   value) and a best-sellers ranking.
4. Input validation, clear error messages and a log file of everything
   that happens.
